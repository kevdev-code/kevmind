# Phase 4: the Brain tab

Status 2026-10-02: in the dashboard, as a third tab next to Live and Memory. It was built as a standalone prototype over seven iterations (this document keeps their decisions and measurements) and then moved into the dashboard; the prototype's folder is now its benchmark harness. The design rules are in [DESIGN.md, section 7](../DESIGN.md).

Open the dashboard and click **Brain**. For checks at a known size, the harness runs the same view on synthetic data:

```bash
node prototype/brain/serve.mjs
```

Then open http://127.0.0.1:4798/ (test port; `?nodes=3000` for the stress case, `?speed=2` for a faster replay, `?nointro`, `?noreplay`, `?bloom=off|light|full|only`). The harness says on the page that its data is synthetic.

Recording: [docs/media/brain-prototype.mp4](media/brain-prototype.mp4) (33 s of the replay at 1280 x 800 with an orbit halfway through, recorded in headless Edge on the real GPU with `prototype/brain/bench/record.mjs`; from iteration 3, before the neon cells).

## Decisions (owner, 2026-10-01)

- Renderer: raw WebGL2. three.js isn't worth 2.1 MB for the same shaders.
- Agents are told from regions by kind, not by hue: subagents are silver-white (marker, beam, trail, tag dot) with their number, Claude is coral, regions are colors. Each agent has a white core and its numbered label.
- Rendering (iteration 3): filaments instead of dots, real neuron shapes, glowing tracts, a cerebellum with folia and Purkinje cells, a quiet overview (labels on hover, zoomed in or with a toggle), a tighter blue/cyan, violet and pink palette, an optional cut, and an intro. The ideas come from screenshots and a video of another "Claude brain" that you shared for reference only; they stay out of the repo (`docs/inspiration/` is git-ignored) and none of their code or assets were used.
- Neon (iteration 6): one saturated hue per lobe (amber, blue, violet, cyan, magenta, green, warm white), checked for color-blind safety; cells as glowing outlines with a bright core; a bloom that only takes what work adds; agent trails; and lobe labels on the brain, right on their regions.
- One animation per kind of action (iteration 9): read, edit, new file, search, command, web, subagent, needs your OK, error and done each have a short figure of their own, built on what the hook events say (an edit's dots only when the lines changed are known), with a legend in both languages; with animations off a cell only lights up. The web's beam is the one signal that leaves the brain.
- Trails through the network (iteration 8): an agent's trip follows real links (fewest hops, strongest first, at most 7), hop by hop, lighting the cells it passes; with no path it glides along the lane between the lobes; no trail ever leaves the brain's volume. The tract bundles are counted from the links that are shown.
- Density and motion (iteration 7): in crowded lobes only the nearer or more important cells are outlines and the rest collapse to small points, so the tissue shows at 3,000 nodes; hot spots are capped and don't add up; a stronger label halo over dense areas; Auto-rotate keeps turning through all activity and labels track it.
- Polish (iteration 4): a slightly longer, fuller front; a slightly bigger, lower cerebellum drawn with folia; nodes as shaded 3D cells per type with a slow, soft breathing; one closing wave in the intro; nodes kept inside the shell (the Cut hides the cut-away half entirely); and density-adaptive light, so dense lobes never stain and the front reads as evenly as the back.
- Keep the tools area in the cerebellum and the "notes citing code" links, as long as they come from real data.
- The brain's well stays dark in the light theme.
- Idle is fully still by default.
- The working project is brighter and the others drop to half; the camera easing toward it is the "Follow" control.
- SwiftShader (Chrome's GPU running on the CPU) is the accepted stand-in for an integrated GPU for now.

## What it shows

**Synthetic data the size of a real workspace.** `demo-agency` (an invented travel agency's app) has 49 memory notes plus `MEMORY.md`, 3 instruction files plus the shared `~/.claude/CLAUDE.md`, 1 Serena note, 346 touched files across nested `frontend/` and `backend/` repos, and 7 tools. With `demo-shop` and `demo-kevmind`: 597 nodes and 773 links. The stress case adds agency-shaped projects up to 3,068 nodes and 4,021 links (10 projects). Names are invented. Generator: `prototype/brain/data.js`.

**A replay of one working session:** Claude reads its instructions and memory, launches three parallel subagents (Explore, general-purpose, code-reviewer) that read, edit, run tests (one fails, then passes) and think (with thinking-token counts), then Claude edits and runs the tests again. 57 s of activity, 12 s of stillness, then it loops.

**A real brain's shape, from four views, all from public-domain plates** (kept with their sources in `docs/reference/`). The side view is a 160 × 160 region map (`prototype/brain/shape.js`) traced from Gray's Anatomy plate 728 (1918), whose lobes are colored, with the brainstem from an 1892 Popular Science Monthly drawing of the whole brain (Gray's lateral plates leave it out). The other three views come from Gray's plates too: a coronal section through the temporal lobes (Gray 718), a hemisphere from above (Gray 725) and the cerebellum's two hemispheres (Gray 703). Each is traced once into an outline profile. Two small adjustments on top (iteration 4): the length is warped smoothly so the frontal lobe is about 7% longer and the rest a little shorter (both ends stay put, so the proportions hold), with a fuller, rounder lower front; and the cerebellum is 12% taller and wider, 6% longer, and hangs a little lower, so more of it shows (a real cerebellum is about a third of the brain's length, which the proportion test keeps). The 3D brain combines them: every column of the side view takes the coronal cross-section, scaled to that column's height and to the top view's width there, so the side, top and front outlines all match their references at once. From the front it is a smooth dome whose sides bulge at the temporal lobes and curve in toward the base; from the top an oval, wider at the back, with the midline fissure along its length; from behind the occipital lobes sit right on the cerebellum's two rounded lobes (a soft W). The fissure is 2.4% of the width; the brainstem is a round tube leaving the base, tilted back. Overlaid at 50% (`prototype/brain/bench/views.mjs`, which also shoots six fixed views at both sizes and measures each outline), height ÷ width is 0.647 against 0.611 for the side (perspective adds a little; orthographic it is 0.63), 0.797 against 0.785 for the front, 1.17 against 1.25 for the top (the plate is a little narrower than the 0.83 width we keep from real-brain averages) and 0.52 against 0.49 for the cerebellum. The surface is a web of filaments: each of the shell's 26,000 points joins its nearest neighbors ahead and behind along its gyrus (the level line of the gyri field) and now and then one in any direction, about 37,000 short, slightly curved strands drawn as instanced ribbons in one draw call, with varying opacity and two tints (a cool blue-gray and a little violet), the points themselves a faint sparkle on top. The Sylvian fissure, the central sulcus and the midline fissure are the clearest grooves; strands never cross the midline. The cerebellum is drawn as folia, stacked leaf lines on its surface (the level lines of the distance from its hilum, where the brainstem joins: arcs that follow the dome from the side, folds that run across it and never cross, closer together toward the rim), in pale ice over a soft glow, with seven Purkinje cells (a soma and a flat fan of branches), and the brainstem's strands run along its axis in blue-white, so each reads as its own structure. As in iteration 2, the shell is brightest where it faces the camera, has a soft, thin edge on the near side's rim and is almost gone behind, so nodes, tracts and pulses stay the brightest things on screen; where agents work, the shell around their lobe brightens softly. Behind it, a deep blue-violet night with a very dim, sparse starfield. Shell, stars and the fixed structures are decoration: never interactive, never counted, never picked. It opens at a 3/4 side view, framed so the whole brain fits with a margin (on a phone too). On a phone (or with `?light`) the view is lighter to save battery: 12,000 shell points (17,000 strands, a little wider), 4 Purkinje cells, 20 frames a second, and fibers with 8 segments instead of 14.

Each lobe holds one kind of knowledge, in both hemispheres, and inside it each region is a project's folder or kind of note, as a 3D volume:

| Lobe | Color | What lives there |
|---|---|---|
| Prefrontal | amber | `CLAUDE.md` files and imports, feedback notes |
| Frontal | blue | docs |
| Parietal (top, back) | violet | server and logic code |
| Occipital (back) | cyan | interface code |
| Temporal (low, on the side) | magenta | auto memory (`MEMORY.md` and notes), Serena notes |
| Cerebellum (under the back) | green | tests and tools (Bash, Grep, Agent, MCP servers) |
| Brainstem | warm white | infrastructure: config, migrations, Docker, `package.json` |

Each lobe is its territory in the region map (prefrontal is the front of the frontal lobe; frontal is its back with the motor strip), through the whole depth and mirrored on both hemispheres, and each lobe's volume is shared among its regions by node count, so a lobe's nodes fill its territory to the edge. A file's lobe comes from its path alone (`lobeOfPath` in `data.js`). Regions go to the hemisphere with less load in their lobe, preferring their project's side, so projects keep a side when they can and a single project still fills both.

**Colors.** The lobe colors are categorical, the brain view's one exemption from DESIGN.md's one-hue-per-meaning rule, confined to the well (the rail's Regions legend is their key). Regions shift slightly around their lobe's hue. Activity never passes for a region: it is always white-cored and labeled. Each lobe has its own saturated hue, about 50 degrees from its neighbors, and a different lightness, so the regions are told apart at a glance and stay apart for color-blind eyes: the closest pair is 14.6 apart (OKLab × 100) with normal vision and 9.0, 7.7 and 7.3 with simulated protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia, against 3.7, 2.3, 2.7 and 2.2 for the pastel palette before; a test keeps it that way, with every label at AA on the night. A lobe's color is on its cells, links, label and haze, and at 30% on the strands of its shell. Claude's coral belongs to Claude alone (its marker, its trail, its thinking breath): no region uses it, and the prefrontal amber sits 52 degrees of hue away. Subagents are silver-white, told apart by their number, so an agent is told from a region by kind, never by hue. Agent beams and heads carry a white core and the chip "#1 Explore · reads …"; the node an agent lands on flashes white and gets a ring in DESIGN.md's state colors (read blue, edit pink, error red, command gray); a touched node keeps a whitened core that cools at 1, 5 and 15 minutes; selection is violet.

**Nodes** are neon cells: the type's shape as a glowing outline in the region's color, hollow, with a bright core: triangles for memory notes, circles for code files, diamonds for instructions, hexagons for Serena notes, two concentric rings for tools. The outline is drawn from the distance to the shape, so it is crisp at any zoom, with a tight glow on both sides; from far away it closes into a point of light in its color. Nearer ones are a little bigger, farther ones smaller and dimmer. Size grows only a little with activity (under 2x), so none dominates; a cell that was just touched fills with light, turns white-hot and gets a ring, then cools back to its color. **Outlines by room:** a lobe's patch of screen holds only so many outlines (about one per 150 px² at overview) before they merge into a painted patch; each lobe's cells are ranked (instructions, then activity) and those beyond its room, the far side first, collapse to small points of their color, so the filaments show through. Closing in gives more room and brings the outlines back; hot, focused and found cells are always outlines. At 600 nodes all are outlines; at 3,000 about 1,250 at overview. Each cell's light breathes slowly and softly, out of step with its neighbors, whenever the brain is drawing anyway; idle stays still. Dendrites and an axon are thin fibers: memory notes and instructions are pyramidal (an apical dendrite toward the surface that forks, two or three basal dendrites, an axon down toward the core); code files, Serena notes and tools are round cells (three or four dendrites around, a shorter axon). Branches stay inside the brain. At overview the main branches are faint and the forks hidden; zoomed in they show in full; a neuron that was just read, edited or run is drawn in full detail and bright, every frame, until its ember cools. **Density-adaptive light:** each lobe's cells, dendrites and haze are lit by a gain from its node density against the average (the occipital lobe packs about ten times the average, the frontal lobes a sixth), so dense lobes have smaller cells and fainter dendrites and haze, sparse ones slightly larger cells: no stain at 3,000 nodes, and the front reads as evenly as the back, with the same nodes and nothing added. The cells' own light goes by how crowded a lobe really is (an outline carries little light): full neon at 600 nodes, dimmer only in the densest lobes at 3,000. Nodes keep 0.04 from the shell in every direction. **Tracts:** the busiest lanes between lobes (the top ten pairs, each at least 3% of the links between lobes) are thick, smooth, glowing bundles of five to eight strands that fan out and fade before the lobes, brighter the more links they carry; the corpus callosum is an arch of eight strands along the midline, brighter the more links cross between hemispheres; the brainstem is a bright warm-white bundle up its axis that fans into both hemispheres. While work goes on, pulses run along the tracts that touch the lobe just worked on. **Links** are curved, glowing fibers colored from one region to the other, low base opacity; links between regions follow one shared lane per pair of lobes (through the lobes they join, arching up and in over the middle inside a hemisphere, crossing between hemispheres along the corpus callosum's arch, dipping toward the core to and from the brainstem; fainter than before, since the tracts carry the structure), so they gather into bundles; co-change links are dashed (a statistic); links around recently touched nodes brighten. **Beams** are glowing tubes (a white core in a wide halo of the agent's hue) that travel the fiber lane between lobes (or arc over the surface inside one) from where an agent was to the file it now reads or edits, the chip riding the head; where they land, the region glows for a moment. **Trails:** an agent's trip runs over real links, hop by hop (or along the lane between lobes when no path exists), always inside the brain; behind the head the way stays as a thinner line in the agent's color and fades over 4.5 s, so an agent's way from file to file can be seen. **Heat and bloom:** the cell an agent is on glows hot, near white at its center, and cools back to its region's color when the agent moves on; hot spots close together merge instead of adding up, and a bloom pass, capped per pixel so it never whites out, makes that light spill: the frame is halved to a quarter size, what the cached brain already holds (shell, tracts, links, ground) is taken out, a threshold keeps only what is bright over an area, and two blurred octaves are added back. So only work blooms, never the shell. It is lighter on phones, off on software renderers (too costly there), and steps down by itself (full, light, off) if the frame rate can't hold with it. **Signals:** while events keep coming, small pulses run along the links of what was just touched. **The lit lobe:** where agents work, the lobe glows in its color and cools with the embers. "Thinks" sends a ripple through nearby nodes in the agent's color, and while Claude thinks a slow warm glow breathes through the whole brain. **Idle** is calm and a little dim; a working session is brighter. Nodes are a little smaller and quieter than the shell, so from afar it reads as a brain first and up close as data. **Depth:** perspective, size attenuation, and fog (far is smaller, dimmer, greyer).

**Labels:** hidden at overview, so only the agent tags show. A lobe's label appears with the pointer over it, all of them when zoomed in, or always with the **Labels** toggle (remembered). On the brain, right on the region it names: centered on the projected center of the lobe's nodes in the hemisphere that faces the camera, with no box and no leader line. Plain text over a dark halo behind the letters only, one line: a dot and the lobe's name in its color, its role dimmer ("● Frontal · docs"). They never overlap: the most important first (where agents work, then size), each takes the free spot nearest its center and never leaves its region; with no room there it is not shown. Its groups (most important first, "+n" for the rest) show under it only for the lobe under the pointer, or for the lobes in the middle of the view when zoomed in. Over a crowded patch of cells their halo is stronger. They fade out only while you drag; when the camera moves by itself (Auto-rotate, Follow, a zoom) they stay and track their regions, gliding to their places. Where agents work the label brightens and the others dim (through their dot; the text stays readable); an agent's tag wins, and a label under one fades. On a phone, one label at most: the lobe where an agent last worked.

**Auto-rotate** (remembered) keeps turning while agents work, events arrive or Follow moves the camera (then it orbits what Follow looks at); only your hand pauses it (a drag, a zoom, a click), and it comes back 3 s after the last touch. It is the one thing that may keep an idle brain moving; with it off, idle is fully still.

**Cut:** a toggle for a midsagittal cutaway: the hemisphere facing the camera loses its cerebrum's shell and its nodes, links and dendrites are hidden, so the inside shows (the corpus callosum's arch, neurons, the brainstem bundle, the cerebellum); it follows the camera across the midline.

**The intro:** once per page load the brain builds itself in about 3 seconds: a seed of light and a ring at the brainstem's base, then a rising wave with a white glow at its front (the brainstem, then the cerebellum, then the cerebrum from its base to its top, nodes and dendrites with their part), the tracts lighting up last with pulses shooting along them, and one brightness wave through everything before it settles and the replay starts. Never with animations off or `prefers-reduced-motion`, never again on returning to the tab; a click, drag, scroll or key skips to the end. It is drawn as a composite: the cached layers once, fully, plus a texture of when the wave reaches each pixel, so each intro frame costs about as much as a still one.

**The activity trace** (bottom left): an EEG-like line of events and a band of thinking tokens over the last 5 minutes, from the replay's events only, with a word on its own row above the line: thinking, working or idle. It scrolls every 2 s while something happened in the last 30 s, then stays still.

**Camera:** full 360-degree orbit (pitch stops short of the poles), inertia when you let go, zoom toward the cursor, Shift-drag or right-drag to pan. The bar: zoom in and out, Fit (back to the opening view; with a node focused, the camera also steps aside by half the card so the card never covers the brain), Re-layout (lays out again what the filters show, behind a short fade), Follow (the camera eases toward where agents work and back to the whole brain when the session is idle) and Auto-rotate (slow turn while idle). Follow and Auto-rotate are off by default.

**Interaction:** hover a node for its name, type, lobe, project, path and stats; click to focus it (neighbors lit, the rest dimmed, a card lists the neighbors as buttons, the keyboard path through the graph); search with `/` (Enter focuses the first match and moves keyboard focus into its neighbors; Esc clears); filters for projects, node types and link types.

**Switches and chrome:** "Animations" off (the default under `prefers-reduced-motion`) draws single static frames: beams land at once, no rings, ripples or inertia. Header, rail and controls are DESIGN.md's. English and Spanish. Below 900 px the rail folds into a "Filters" disclosure, below 640 px the trace shrinks to its word and line and moves with the camera bar under the title, below 520 px chips shrink to "#n".

## Renderer: raw WebGL2 (decided)

Measured on the iteration-1 scene at commit `e753723` (same 3,068 nodes and 4,021 links, full redraw every frame; reproduce with `git checkout e753723 -- prototype/brain`):

| | Canvas 2D | Raw WebGL2 | three.js 0.186 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Code to ship | 6.6 KB | 18 KB | 2,121 KB (417 KB gzip) |
| RTX 3080 Ti, 30 fps: script per frame / GPU process | 3.85 ms / 68% | 0.19 ms / 5% | 0.25 ms / 5% |
| RTX 3080 Ti uncapped | 47 fps | 120 fps (display limit) | 120 fps |
| SwiftShader, 30 fps cap | 0.4 fps | 28.8 fps | 28.8 fps |

three.js ran the same shaders, so it costs the same per frame; it would only add 2.1 MB to a package that is 0.3 MB today. Canvas 2D fails the budget. The renderer today is `gl.js`, 39 KB (13 KB gzip).

- **Two caches.** The deep cache (ground, shell, dendrites, tracts) is redrawn only when the camera or what is shown changes (filters, focus, search, the cut); the main cache (the deep one plus haze and links) also when the activity lighting changes (0.5 rebuilds per second during the replay). Each frame copies the main cache and draws the glow, the active neurons, nodes, beams and sprites on top. Activity never redraws the tens of thousands of strands and dendrite segments: the neurons just touched are drawn on their own, every frame (at most 40), and a busy lobe's glow uses the shell's points, not its strands.
- **No instancing.** The first version of this round drew strands and fiber segments as instances and fell to 3.4 fps on SwiftShader's replay and 2.3 rotating at 3,068 nodes. Profiling by layer showed the cost was per instance (about 5–6 µs each on SwiftShader, whatever its size: 7,400 short strands cost 49 ms a frame for 30,000 vertices). Strands and segments are now pulled from float textures by vertex id and drawn as plain triangles, which is what made SwiftShader faster than before; on the RTX it changes nothing. While the camera moves, strands and links draw straight (one segment each); everything else stays, so nothing pops when it stops.
- **The intro** draws the caches once, in full, plus a texture of when the rising wave reaches each pixel; each intro frame composites them and draws the nodes and the tracts on their own clock, so it costs about a still frame. Its clock starts after the first two frames, so the one long frame (drawing everything once: about 200 ms on SwiftShader, which is the "longest wait") falls before anything animates. On SwiftShader's 597-node run, which was that browser's first load, one more long frame (230 ms) came during the intro; on the second load (3,068 nodes) it didn't, and the RTX never showed one, so it looks like a one-time warm-up of the software renderer, though I didn't pin it down.
- **First frame** grew by 50–135 ms on the RTX and 75 ms at 3,068 nodes on SwiftShader: building the strands (about 120 ms for 26,000 points), the dendrites and the tracts. On SwiftShader at 597 nodes it got faster, since the first frame no longer pays for instancing.
- **Auto-rotate on SwiftShader** at 3,068 nodes is the weakest case, 22.9 fps (21.6 before): the cached layers are redrawn every frame. On a phone the view is lighter (12,000 shell points, 20 fps cap).
- Two benchmark artifacts, fixed: the first Windows GPU-counter query of a run stalls the browser's frames for several seconds (the benchmark warms it up in a throwaway launch); and a fresh Edge profile still signs into the Windows account and syncs its extensions, whose welcome tabs take the foreground a few seconds in and throttle the measured page to 1 fps at random. The harness now launches Edge with sync and extensions off. A third, found this round: closing a test browser only killed Edge's launcher, which had already handed off to the real browser, so browsers piled up (219 processes) and later launches on the same port connected to an old browser in the wrong GPU mode (a "SwiftShader" run measured the RTX). The harness now closes the browser over DevTools and waits for its port to be free; every number on this page comes from the fixed harness.

## Integrated GPUs: SwiftShader is the stand-in

This machine has no integrated GPU enabled (the Ryzen 9 9950X3D's Radeon graphics is off; only the RTX 3080 Ti shows up), so the numbers above use SwiftShader, which you accepted as the stand-in. SwiftShader is harsher than an integrated GPU in a specific way: it pays per triangle on the CPU, which a real GPU does in hardware. At 3,000 nodes it holds 29 fps on the replay and 23 fps while rotating, using many CPU cores to do it. Running `node prototype/brain/bench/run.mjs out.json` on a laptop with an integrated GPU would replace the stand-in with real numbers before integrating.

## The links, and what is scenery

Six kinds of link, each a real relation read from your files or from what Claude did. A link is drawn only between two nodes.

| Link | From, to | Built from |
|---|---|---|
| Links between notes | note to note | A `[[name]]` in a memory note, or a `mem:name` in a Serena note, that names another note of the same project. They are the Memory tab's links; a link to no note is a problem there and nothing here. |
| Index entries | `MEMORY.md` to note | Each note `MEMORY.md` lists. |
| Imports | file to file | An `@path` import between instruction files, and the import statements of code files (below). |
| Notes citing code | note or instruction file to file | A file path written in the note's text that is there in the working tree. Paths in code blocks, in negated sentences and under "don't" headings are skipped, as in the Memory tab. A cited file is a node even when Claude never touched it. |
| Changed together | file and file | The experience tools' answer: edited in the same work episode at least 3 times, on at least 2 days, in at least half of the file's edit episodes; or committed together in git (from `git log`, at its own thresholds). A hub that changes with everything is left out. |
| Read before edit | file to file | Also the experience tools': read before the file's first edit in at least 3 episodes, on at least 2 days, in at least 60% of its edit episodes. |

**Imports of code files** (`src/imports.js`). For every code file that is a node, its import statements are read (only those: no symbols, no index) and each one is resolved to a file the way its language does. When that file is a node too, it is a link; an import never adds a node. What is not a file of the project is no link: an npm or pub package, the standard library, a commented-out line.

| Language | What is read | How it finds the file |
|---|---|---|
| JavaScript, TypeScript (`.js .jsx .mjs .cjs .ts .tsx .mts .cts`, and the scripts of `.vue .svelte .astro`) | `import … from`, `import '…'`, `export … from`, `import('…')`, `require('…')` | Relative paths with or without extension (`./x.js` written for `x.ts`), a folder's `index` file (a barrel is a file: the importer links to it, and it links to what it re-exports), aliases from the nearest `tsconfig.json` or `jsconfig.json` (`paths`, `baseUrl`, what it `extends` or references, comments allowed), and `@/` or `~/` as the package's `src` when no config says. |
| Dart | `import`, `export`, `part`, `part of`, conditional imports | Relative paths, and `package:name/…` when `name` is the one in the nearest `pubspec.yaml` (its `lib` folder). |
| Python | `import a.b`, `from a.b import c`, `from . import x`, `from ..pkg import (y, z)` | Dots are relative; otherwise the nearest folder above the file (or its `src`) that holds the package. `from pkg import name` goes to the submodule when there is one, else to the package. |
| PHP | `use A\B\C;` (grouped, aliased), `require`/`include` | `composer.json`'s PSR-4 folders (`App\` is `app/`), and paths relative to the file. |
| C# | `using N;`, `using static N.T;`, `using X = N.T;`, the file's own namespace | A `using` names a namespace, not a file, so the link goes to the files that declare that namespace and whose type (the file's name) the importing file mentions. |
| CSS, Sass, Less | `@import`, `@use`, `@forward` | Relative paths, Sass partials (`_name.scss`). |
| HTML | `<script src>`, `<link href>` | Relative paths, and `/x` from a folder above the page (or its `public` folder). |

Not read yet: Go, Rust, Java, Kotlin, Swift, Ruby, C and C++. Also not followed: packages of a monorepo imported by name (`@scope/pkg`), `package.json` `imports` that no tsconfig repeats, and C#'s global and implicit usings. The styles above are the cases of `test/imports.test.mjs`.

**Scenery, never counted:** the shell's filaments and junctions, each cell's dendrites and axon, the ground and the stars, the corpus callosum's arch, the brainstem's bundle and the cerebellum's Purkinje cells. **Tracts are the real links again, added up:** the links between two lobes that are shown (by the link, project and node-type filters) are counted, and the busiest lanes (at most 10, each with at least 3 links and 3% of the links between lobes) are drawn as a bundle whose thickness and brightness follow the count. A bundle's strands are its shape, not one per link. Hide a kind of link and the bundles are counted again without it: with only "Read before edit" shown (1 link here) no bundle is drawn. The corpus callosum is always drawn; its brightness follows the share of links that cross between hemispheres, and it and the brainstem's bundle dim (down to 30%) when few links are shown, so they are not read as bundles of links. With a filter on, "Filters active · Reset" shows next to the counts.

**On real data** (this machine, 2026-10-02): 282 nodes and 453 links: 113 changed together, 97 citations, 98 imports (KevMind 74, a second workspace 24, a third none: its 6 code nodes don't import each other), 75 index entries, 69 links between notes, 1 read before edit. Imports were 0 before: only `@path` imports between instruction files were links, and no `CLAUDE.md` here uses them. Read before edit is rare by its thresholds: of the 479 pairs (a file read, another edited later in the episode) in KevMind's 38 edit episodes, 469 happened in fewer than 3 episodes, 7 on a single day and 2 in under 60% of the file's edit episodes; one passes (`src/server.js` before `bin/kevmind.js`). The other two workspaces have 2 to 3 edit episodes each, too few for any pattern. Most "changed together" links here come from git (the episodes alone give fewer than 20).

**What reading imports costs.** On the real graph's 177 files: 34 ms the first time, 16 ms after (what a file imports is remembered until the file changes; folders are listed once per build). As a stress test, over every source file of a workspace: 1,086 files (TypeScript) in 1.1 s, then 0.13 s; 2,807 files (Dart and PHP) in 1.5 s, then 0.21 s. The scan yields every 100 files, so the server keeps answering hooks meanwhile. The response grew from 68 KB to 71 KB. The view draws 453 links instead of 355; nothing in the renderer changed (the harness runs it at 773 and 4,021 links).

## How it is integrated

1. **Server.** `GET /api/brain` (`src/brain.js`) builds one graph for the projects seen in sessions (the 12 most recent), from what KevMind already has: the memory report of each (instruction files and their imports, `MEMORY.md` and its notes, their links, what they cite, Serena's notes), the import statements of the code files in the graph (`src/imports.js`), the experience aggregate (the files Claude read and edited in the last 90 days, files edited together and files read before an edit, at the existing thresholds) and how often each tool was used and failed (counted from the logs; every tool of an MCP server is one node). A repo nested in a workspace (a `frontend/` with its own `.git`) is part of that workspace: one project, each file once. A file is one node whoever mentions it. At most 1,200 code files per project and 4,000 nodes in all; the most active are kept. Cached for 20 s. It only reads: `test/brain.test.mjs` fails if `src/brain.js` could write a file or start a process.
2. **Events.** A read or edit event now carries its file, relative to the session's folder (`src/state.js`), so the page finds its node; the feed still shows only the name. A search (Grep, Glob) says where it looked. When a call ends, what it did is written on its own event (`outcomeOf`): the lines an edit added and removed (from the patch Claude Code reports), that a file was created, the files a search matched (24 paths at most, kept on the 3 newest searches only, so the session's summary stays small), how long a command ran. Numbers and paths only, never content, and measured by the server before the payload is redacted and cut (a spooled or older event whose lists were cut gives no count rather than a short one). The page plays each action's figure from that; a file that was just created makes it ask for a graph newer than the event (`/api/brain?after=`, rebuilt at most every 5 s), so its cell is born 2.5 to 8 s later. "Needs your OK" now ends when the call that asked runs, not at the next call.
3. **Page.** A third view button. Nothing of the view loads until it is first opened: `public/brain.js` (9 KB of glue, loaded with the page) then loads `public/brain/brain.css` and imports `public/brain/view.js`, fetches the graph, places each node in a lobe and a region (`graph.js`) and mounts the view. It follows the session selected in Live: what already happened is applied at once (agents where they are, what was touched still warm by its age, the trace's last five minutes), and each new event plays (a read or an edit goes to its file, a command to its tool's node, an error flashes where the agent is, thinking ripples; a subagent appears with its number and type). A burst, or what piled up while another view was shown, is applied at once too. When Claude touches a file the graph doesn't have, the page asks for a newer graph (6 to 30 s later) and rebuilds the view where it was: same camera, no intro.
4. **Views and themes.** Live and Memory are untouched: with the Brain tab shown, Live stops rendering (it catches up when shown again), and the brain stops while another view or tab is shown. The rail follows the page's theme; the well is always dark. The styles are scoped to `.brain-view`, so nothing leaks either way.
5. **Phone.** "View on phone" serves the tab like the rest, read-only: the graph, the code and the styles need the link's token (`test/share.test.mjs`). A phone gets the lighter brain (fewer shell points, 20 fps, the tight bloom only) and one region label at most.
6. **Harness.** `prototype/brain/` mounts the same `view.js` on synthetic data with a replay, for the benchmark and for screenshots at ~600 and ~3,000 nodes.
