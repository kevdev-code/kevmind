# Phase 4: the brain view (prototype)

Status 2026-10-02: iteration 4 of a standalone prototype (a polish round on top of the rendering round), for review. Nothing in the dashboard changed; integration waits for your OK. The design rules are in [DESIGN.md, section 7](../DESIGN.md).

```bash
node prototype/brain/serve.mjs
```

Then open http://127.0.0.1:4798/ (test port; `?nodes=3000` for the stress case, `?speed=2` for a faster replay). The data is synthetic and labeled so on the page.

Recording: [docs/media/brain-prototype.mp4](media/brain-prototype.mp4) (33 s of the replay at 1280 x 800 with an orbit halfway through, recorded in headless Edge on the real GPU with `prototype/brain/bench/record.mjs`).

## Decisions (owner, 2026-10-01)

- Renderer: raw WebGL2. three.js isn't worth 2.1 MB for the same shaders.
- Agent beams in color (cyan, orchid, ice blue; Claude coral), each with a white core and its numbered label, so they never pass for a region color. No orange.
- Rendering (iteration 3): filaments instead of dots, real neuron shapes, glowing tracts, a cerebellum with folia and Purkinje cells, a quiet overview (labels on hover, zoomed in or with a toggle), a tighter blue/cyan, violet and pink palette, an optional cut, and an intro. The ideas come from screenshots and a video of another "Claude brain" that you shared for reference only; they stay out of the repo (`docs/inspiration/` is git-ignored) and none of their code or assets were used.
- Neon (iteration 6): one saturated hue per lobe (amber, blue, violet, cyan, magenta, green, warm white), checked for color-blind safety; cells as glowing outlines with a bright core; a bloom that only takes what work adds; agent trails; and lobe labels on the brain, right on their regions.
- Polish (iteration 4): a slightly longer, fuller front; a slightly bigger, lower cerebellum drawn with folia; nodes as shaded 3D cells per type with a slow, soft breathing; one closing wave in the intro; nodes kept inside the shell (the Cut hides the cut-away half entirely); and density-adaptive light, so dense lobes never stain and the front reads as evenly as the back.
- Keep the tools area in the cerebellum and the "notes citing code" links, as long as they come from real data.
- The brain's well stays dark in the light theme.
- Idle is fully still by default.
- The working project is brighter and the others drop to half; the camera easing toward it is the "Follow" control.
- SwiftShader (Chrome's GPU running on the CPU) is the accepted stand-in for an integrated GPU for now.

## What it shows

**Synthetic data shaped like your projects.** `demo-clinic` is OdonMind-sized (its real memory report: 51 notes, 4 instruction files, 1 Serena note, nested repos): 49 memory notes plus `MEMORY.md`, 3 instruction files plus the shared `~/.claude/CLAUDE.md`, 1 Serena note, 346 touched files across nested `frontend/` and `backend/` repos, and 7 tools. With `demo-shop` and `demo-kevmind`: 597 nodes and 773 links. The stress case adds clinic-shaped projects up to 3,068 nodes and 4,021 links (10 projects). Names are invented. Generator: `prototype/brain/data.js`.

**A replay of one working session:** Claude reads its instructions and memory, launches three parallel subagents (Explore, general-purpose, code-reviewer) that read, edit, run tests (one fails, then passes) and think (with thinking-token counts), then Claude edits and runs the tests again. 57 s of activity, 12 s of stillness, then it loops.

**A real brain's shape, from four views, all from public-domain plates** (kept with their sources in `docs/reference/`). The side view is a 160 × 160 region map (`prototype/brain/shape.js`) traced from Gray's Anatomy plate 728 (1918), whose lobes are colored, with the brainstem from an 1892 Popular Science Monthly drawing of the whole brain (Gray's lateral plates leave it out). The other three views come from Gray's plates too: a coronal section through the temporal lobes (Gray 718), a hemisphere from above (Gray 725) and the cerebellum's two hemispheres (Gray 703). Each is traced once into an outline profile. Two small adjustments on top (iteration 4): the length is warped smoothly so the frontal lobe is about 7% longer and the rest a little shorter (both ends stay put, so the proportions hold), with a fuller, rounder lower front; and the cerebellum is 12% taller and wider, 6% longer, and hangs a little lower, so more of it shows (a real cerebellum is about a third of the brain's length, which the proportion test keeps). The 3D brain combines them: every column of the side view takes the coronal cross-section, scaled to that column's height and to the top view's width there, so the side, top and front outlines all match their references at once. From the front it is a smooth dome whose sides bulge at the temporal lobes and curve in toward the base; from the top an oval, wider at the back, with the midline fissure along its length; from behind the occipital lobes sit right on the cerebellum's two rounded lobes (a soft W). The fissure is 2.4% of the width; the brainstem is a round tube leaving the base, tilted back. Overlaid at 50% (`prototype/brain/bench/views.mjs`, which also shoots six fixed views at both sizes and measures each outline), height ÷ width is 0.647 against 0.611 for the side (perspective adds a little; orthographic it is 0.63), 0.797 against 0.785 for the front, 1.17 against 1.25 for the top (the plate is a little narrower than the 0.83 width we keep from real-brain averages) and 0.52 against 0.49 for the cerebellum. The surface is a web of filaments: each of the shell's 26,000 points joins its nearest neighbors ahead and behind along its gyrus (the level line of the gyri field) and now and then one in any direction, about 37,000 short, slightly curved strands drawn as instanced ribbons in one draw call, with varying opacity and two tints (a cool blue-gray and a little violet), the points themselves a faint sparkle on top. The Sylvian fissure, the central sulcus and the midline fissure are the clearest grooves; strands never cross the midline. The cerebellum is drawn as folia, stacked leaf lines on its surface (the level lines of the distance from its hilum, where the brainstem joins: arcs that follow the dome from the side, folds that run across it and never cross, closer together toward the rim), in pale ice over a soft glow, with seven Purkinje cells (a soma and a flat fan of branches), and the brainstem's strands run along its axis in blue-white, so each reads as its own structure. As in iteration 2, the shell is brightest where it faces the camera, has a soft, thin edge on the near side's rim and is almost gone behind, so nodes, tracts and pulses stay the brightest things on screen; where agents work, the shell around their lobe brightens softly. Behind it, a deep blue-violet night with a very dim, sparse starfield. Shell, stars and the fixed structures are decoration: never interactive, never counted, never picked. It opens at a 3/4 side view, framed so the whole brain fits with a margin (on a phone too). On a phone (or with `?light`) the view is lighter to save battery: 12,000 shell points (17,000 strands, a little wider), 4 Purkinje cells, 20 frames a second, and fibers with 8 segments instead of 14.

Each lobe holds one kind of knowledge, in both hemispheres, and inside it each region is a project's folder or kind of note, as a 3D volume:

| Lobe | Color | What lives there |
|---|---|---|
| Prefrontal | amber | `CLAUDE.md` files and imports, feedback notes |
| Frontal | blue | docs |
| Parietal (top, back) | violet | server and logic code |
| Occipital (back) | cyan | interface code |
| Temporal (low, on the side) | magenta | auto memory (`MEMORY.md` and notes), Serena notes |
| Cerebellum (under the back) | green | tests and tools (Bash, Grep, Agent, MCP servers) |
| Brainstem | warm white | infrastructure: config, migrations, Docker, `package.json` |

Each lobe is its territory in the region map (prefrontal is the front of the frontal lobe; frontal is its back with the motor strip), through the whole depth and mirrored on both hemispheres, and each lobe's volume is shared among its regions by node count, so a lobe's nodes fill its territory to the edge. A file's lobe comes from its path alone (`lobeOfPath` in `data.js`). Regions go to the hemisphere with less load in their lobe, preferring their project's side, so projects keep a side when they can and a single project still fills both.

**Colors.** The lobe colors are categorical, the brain view's one exemption from DESIGN.md's one-hue-per-meaning rule, confined to the well (the rail's Regions legend is their key). Regions shift slightly around their lobe's hue. Activity never passes for a region: it is always white-cored and labeled. Each lobe has its own saturated hue, about 50 degrees from its neighbors, and a different lightness, so the regions are told apart at a glance and stay apart for color-blind eyes: the closest pair is 14.6 apart (OKLab × 100) with normal vision and 9.0, 7.7 and 7.3 with simulated protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia, against 3.7, 2.3, 2.7 and 2.2 for the pastel palette before; a test keeps it that way, with every label at AA on the night. A lobe's color is on its cells, links, label and haze, and at 30% on the strands of its shell. Claude's coral belongs to Claude alone (its marker, its trail, its thinking breath): no region uses it, and the prefrontal amber sits 52 degrees of hue away. Subagents are a pale cyan, orchid and ice blue, lighter and less saturated than any region. Agent beams and heads carry a white core and the chip "#1 Explore · reads …"; the node an agent lands on flashes white and gets a ring in DESIGN.md's state colors (read blue, edit pink, error red, command gray); a touched node keeps a whitened core that cools at 1, 5 and 15 minutes; selection is violet.

**Nodes** are neon cells: the type's shape as a glowing outline in the region's color, hollow, with a bright core: triangles for memory notes, circles for code files, diamonds for instructions, hexagons for Serena notes, two concentric rings for tools. The outline is drawn from the distance to the shape, so it is crisp at any zoom, with a tight glow on both sides; from far away it closes into a point of light in its color. Nearer ones are a little bigger, farther ones smaller and dimmer. Size grows only a little with activity (under 2x), so none dominates; a cell that was just touched fills with light, turns white-hot and gets a ring, then cools back to its color. Each cell's light breathes slowly and softly, out of step with its neighbors, whenever the brain is drawing anyway; idle stays still. Dendrites and an axon are thin fibers: memory notes and instructions are pyramidal (an apical dendrite toward the surface that forks, two or three basal dendrites, an axon down toward the core); code files, Serena notes and tools are round cells (three or four dendrites around, a shorter axon). Branches stay inside the brain. At overview the main branches are faint and the forks hidden; zoomed in they show in full; a neuron that was just read, edited or run is drawn in full detail and bright, every frame, until its ember cools. **Density-adaptive light:** each lobe's cells, dendrites and haze are lit by a gain from its node density against the average (the occipital lobe packs about ten times the average, the frontal lobes a sixth), so dense lobes have smaller cells and fainter dendrites and haze, sparse ones slightly larger cells: no stain at 3,000 nodes, and the front reads as evenly as the back, with the same nodes and nothing added. The cells' own light goes by how crowded a lobe really is (an outline carries little light): full neon at 600 nodes, dimmer only in the densest lobes at 3,000. Nodes keep 0.04 from the shell in every direction. **Tracts:** the busiest lanes between lobes (the top ten pairs, each at least 3% of the links between lobes) are thick, smooth, glowing bundles of five to eight strands that fan out and fade before the lobes, brighter the more links they carry; the corpus callosum is an arch of eight strands along the midline, brighter the more links cross between hemispheres; the brainstem is a bright warm-white bundle up its axis that fans into both hemispheres. While work goes on, pulses run along the tracts that touch the lobe just worked on. **Links** are curved, glowing fibers colored from one region to the other, low base opacity; links between regions follow one shared lane per pair of lobes (through the lobes they join, arching up and in over the middle inside a hemisphere, crossing between hemispheres along the corpus callosum's arch, dipping toward the core to and from the brainstem; fainter than before, since the tracts carry the structure), so they gather into bundles; co-change links are dashed (a statistic); links around recently touched nodes brighten. **Beams** are glowing tubes (a white core in a wide halo of the agent's hue) that travel the fiber lane between lobes (or arc over the surface inside one) from where an agent was to the file it now reads or edits, the chip riding the head; where they land, the region glows for a moment. **Trails:** behind the head the whole path stays as a thinner arc in the agent's color and fades over 4.5 s, so an agent's way from file to file can be seen. **Heat and bloom:** the cell an agent is on glows hot, near white at its center, and cools back to its region's color when the agent moves on; a bloom pass makes that light spill: the frame is halved to a quarter size, what the cached brain already holds (shell, tracts, links, ground) is taken out, a threshold keeps only what is bright over an area, and two blurred octaves are added back. So only work blooms, never the shell. It is lighter on phones, off on software renderers (too costly there), and steps down by itself (full, light, off) if the frame rate can't hold with it. **Signals:** while events keep coming, small pulses run along the links of what was just touched. **The lit lobe:** where agents work, the lobe glows in its color and cools with the embers. "Thinks" sends a ripple through nearby nodes in the agent's color, and while Claude thinks a slow warm glow breathes through the whole brain. **Idle** is calm and a little dim; a working session is brighter. Nodes are a little smaller and quieter than the shell, so from afar it reads as a brain first and up close as data. **Depth:** perspective, size attenuation, and fog (far is smaller, dimmer, greyer).

**Labels:** hidden at overview, so only the agent tags show. A lobe's label appears with the pointer over it, all of them when zoomed in, or always with the **Labels** toggle (remembered). On the brain, right on the region it names: centered on the projected center of the lobe's nodes in the hemisphere that faces the camera, with no box and no leader line. Plain text over a dark halo behind the letters only, one line: a dot and the lobe's name in its color, its role dimmer ("● Frontal · docs"). They never overlap: the most important first (where agents work, then size), each takes the free spot nearest its center and never leaves its region; with no room there it is not shown. Its groups (most important first, "+n" for the rest) show under it only for the lobe under the pointer, or for the lobes in the middle of the view when zoomed in. They fade out while the camera moves and come back when it stops. Where agents work the label brightens and the others dim (through their dot; the text stays readable); an agent's tag wins, and a label under one fades. On a phone, one label at most: the lobe where an agent last worked.

**Cut:** a toggle for a midsagittal cutaway: the hemisphere facing the camera loses its cerebrum's shell and its nodes, links and dendrites are hidden, so the inside shows (the corpus callosum's arch, neurons, the brainstem bundle, the cerebellum); it follows the camera across the midline.

**The intro:** once per page load the brain builds itself in about 3 seconds: a seed of light and a ring at the brainstem's base, then a rising wave with a white glow at its front (the brainstem, then the cerebellum, then the cerebrum from its base to its top, nodes and dendrites with their part), the tracts lighting up last with pulses shooting along them, and one brightness wave through everything before it settles and the replay starts. Never with animations off or `prefers-reduced-motion`, never again on returning to the tab; a click, drag, scroll or key skips to the end. It is drawn as a composite: the cached layers once, fully, plus a texture of when the wave reaches each pixel, so each intro frame costs about as much as a still one.

**The activity trace** (bottom left): an EEG-like line of events and a band of thinking tokens over the last 5 minutes, from the replay's events only, with a word on its own row above the line: thinking, working or idle. It scrolls every 2 s while something happened in the last 30 s, then stays still.

**Camera:** full 360-degree orbit (pitch stops short of the poles), inertia when you let go, zoom toward the cursor, Shift-drag or right-drag to pan. The bar: zoom in and out, Fit (back to the opening view; with a node focused, the camera also steps aside by half the card so the card never covers the brain), Re-layout (lays out again what the filters show, behind a short fade), Follow (the camera eases toward where agents work and back to the whole brain when the session is idle) and Auto-rotate (slow turn while idle). Follow and Auto-rotate are off by default.

**Interaction:** hover a node for its name, type, lobe, project, path and stats; click to focus it (neighbors lit, the rest dimmed, a card lists the neighbors as buttons, the keyboard path through the graph); search with `/` (Enter focuses the first match and moves keyboard focus into its neighbors; Esc clears); filters for projects, node types and link types.

**Switches and chrome:** "Animations" off (the default under `prefers-reduced-motion`) draws single static frames: beams land at once, no rings, ripples or inertia. Header, rail and controls are DESIGN.md's. English and Spanish. Below 900 px the rail folds into a "Filters" disclosure, below 640 px the trace shrinks to its word and line and moves with the camera bar under the title, below 520 px chips shrink to "#n".

## Renderer: raw WebGL2 (decided)

Measured on the iteration-1 scene at commit `e753723` (same 3,068 nodes and 4,021 links, full redraw every frame; reproduce with `git checkout e753723 -- prototype/brain`):

| | Canvas 2D | Raw WebGL2 | three.js 0.186 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Code to ship | 6.6 KB | 18 KB | 2,121 KB (417 KB gzip) |
| RTX 3080 Ti, 30 fps: script per frame / GPU process | 3.85 ms / 68% | 0.19 ms / 5% | 0.25 ms / 5% |
| RTX 3080 Ti uncapped | 47 fps | 120 fps (display limit) | 120 fps |
| SwiftShader, 30 fps cap | 0.4 fps | 28.8 fps | 28.8 fps |

three.js ran the same shaders, so it costs the same per frame; it would only add 2.1 MB to a package that is 0.3 MB today. Canvas 2D fails the budget. The renderer today is `gl.js`, 39 KB (13 KB gzip).

The bloom itself, measured on SwiftShader during the replay at 597 nodes: 279% of a core without it, 430% with its tight octave, 443% with both, at the same 28.8 fps; so a software renderer gets none (`?bloom=light` or `full` turns it on for a check). Hidden tab and the other view: 0 fps, as before.

- **Two caches.** The deep cache (ground, shell, dendrites, tracts) is redrawn only when the camera or what is shown changes (filters, focus, search, the cut); the main cache (the deep one plus haze and links) also when the activity lighting changes (0.5 rebuilds per second during the replay). Each frame copies the main cache and draws the glow, the active neurons, nodes, beams and sprites on top. Activity never redraws the tens of thousands of strands and dendrite segments: the neurons just touched are drawn on their own, every frame (at most 40), and a busy lobe's glow uses the shell's points, not its strands.
- **No instancing.** The first version of this round drew strands and fiber segments as instances and fell to 3.4 fps on SwiftShader's replay and 2.3 rotating at 3,068 nodes. Profiling by layer showed the cost was per instance (about 5–6 µs each on SwiftShader, whatever its size: 7,400 short strands cost 49 ms a frame for 30,000 vertices). Strands and segments are now pulled from float textures by vertex id and drawn as plain triangles, which is what made SwiftShader faster than before; on the RTX it changes nothing. While the camera moves, strands and links draw straight (one segment each); everything else stays, so nothing pops when it stops.
- **The intro** draws the caches once, in full, plus a texture of when the rising wave reaches each pixel; each intro frame composites them and draws the nodes and the tracts on their own clock, so it costs about a still frame. Its clock starts after the first two frames, so the one long frame (drawing everything once: about 200 ms on SwiftShader, which is the "longest wait") falls before anything animates. On SwiftShader's 597-node run, which was that browser's first load, one more long frame (230 ms) came during the intro; on the second load (3,068 nodes) it didn't, and the RTX never showed one, so it looks like a one-time warm-up of the software renderer, though I didn't pin it down.
- **First frame** grew by 50–135 ms on the RTX and 75 ms at 3,068 nodes on SwiftShader: building the strands (about 120 ms for 26,000 points), the dendrites and the tracts. On SwiftShader at 597 nodes it got faster, since the first frame no longer pays for instancing.
- **Auto-rotate on SwiftShader** at 3,068 nodes is the weakest case, 22.9 fps (21.6 before): the cached layers are redrawn every frame. On a phone the view is lighter (12,000 shell points, 20 fps cap).
- Two benchmark artifacts, fixed: the first Windows GPU-counter query of a run stalls the browser's frames for several seconds (the benchmark warms it up in a throwaway launch); and a fresh Edge profile still signs into the Windows account and syncs its extensions, whose welcome tabs take the foreground a few seconds in and throttle the measured page to 1 fps at random. The harness now launches Edge with sync and extensions off. A third, found this round: closing a test browser only killed Edge's launcher, which had already handed off to the real browser, so browsers piled up (219 processes) and later launches on the same port connected to an old browser in the wrong GPU mode (a "SwiftShader" run measured the RTX). The harness now closes the browser over DevTools and waits for its port to be free; every number on this page comes from the fixed harness.

## Integrated GPUs: SwiftShader is the stand-in

This machine has no integrated GPU enabled (the Ryzen 9 9950X3D's Radeon graphics is off; only the RTX 3080 Ti shows up), so the numbers above use SwiftShader, which you accepted as the stand-in. SwiftShader is harsher than an integrated GPU in a specific way: it pays per triangle on the CPU, which a real GPU does in hardware. At 3,000 nodes it holds 29 fps on the replay and 23 fps while rotating, using many CPU cores to do it. Running `node prototype/brain/bench/run.mjs out.json` on a laptop with an integrated GPU would replace the stand-in with real numbers before integrating.

## Integration plan (after your OK)

1. Server: `GET /api/brain?key=<project>|all` builds the graph from what KevMind already has: the memory report (instructions and imports, notes, `MEMORY.md` index, links, cites, Serena), the experience aggregate (touched files with reads and edits from episodes, co-change pairs from episodes and git through `partners`, read-before-edit through `readFirst`, with the existing thresholds), and tool counts from the logs. Cached per project and rebuilt when those change. Read-only, like the Memory tab.
2. Client: `public/brain.js` (the renderer from `gl.js` plus the view) behind a third view button. Live activity from the existing `/stream` events: `read`, `edit`, `command`, `error`, `thinks`, `agent_start`, `agent_done` map one-to-one to the replay's kinds; the trace uses the event rate and the transcript's thinking tokens. Layout runs on load (about 50 ms at 600 nodes and 160 ms at 3,000 in Node) or in a worker if real projects get bigger.
3. Settle the breakpoints with the dashboard's (720, 900, 1,100 px) and move the brain's tokens from `prototype/brain/style.css` into `public/style.css`.
4. The benchmark and `test/brain-prototype.test.mjs` move with it.
